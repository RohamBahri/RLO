# RLO research principles

These are **non-negotiable design requirements** for new methods, not claims already demonstrated by the old checkpoints.

1. **Learn across instances.** Train **one shared policy** from many independently generated instances, with a range of sizes and problem parameters (initially demands and capacity). The method must work on **unseen instances**, including held-out sizes and changed parameter regimes. A strong result on one repeatedly optimized instance is not enough. Extensions to new constraint *types* must be tested explicitly; varying capacity alone does not establish that ability.
2. **Learn from experience, not demonstrations.** Use problem definitions, legal-action rules, objectives/rewards, and the agent's own rollouts. **No expert/solver demonstrations, heuristic tours, externally prescribed actions, or teacher labels.** The learner may derive advantages and KL-regularized policy-improvement targets solely from its own simulated returns; projecting the policy toward those experience-derived targets is allowed. Exact or heuristic solver outputs cannot enter these targets. A value function may regress on returns/TD targets derived from the agent's own experience; this is an RL learning signal, not an external teaching dataset.
3. **Keep evaluation separate.** Exact solvers, PyVRP, OR-Tools, Clarke–Wright and frozen reference tours are **benchmarks only**. Never import these solutions or their next-action labels into training. They may be used after training to measure quality. Feasibility masks derived from the mathematical constraints are permitted; heuristic advice is not.
4. **Measure generalization and compute.** Report feasibility, cost, variability across independent seeds, and training/inference effort. Compare in-distribution versus unseen sizes and shifted capacities/demands. Keep validation for choices and hold out final test instances. Compare against strong nonlearned and RL baselines.
5. **Stay lean.** Prefer small, independently testable components. A method or feature must answer a defined research question before adding complexity.

**Implication for MBPI:** Rollout-based KL improvement and cross-entropy/KL projections are permitted only when targets derive from the current learner's own simulated returns. Expert, solver, and heuristic labels remain prohibited. Exact-oracle values are evaluation diagnostics, not training targets.

**Literature positioning:** RL without expert labels and generalization across sizes have prior art (e.g., [POMO](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/hash/f231f2107df69eab0a3862d50018a9b2-Abstract.html), [TTPL](https://proceedings.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/6edd46d69ef91f4555d67f7b321d6902-Abstract-Conference.html), [ASAP](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/fang26t.html)). Our intended contribution is stronger, systematic generalization **together with** experience-only learning and efficient computation, which must be supported empirically rather than asserted as absent from existing literature.
